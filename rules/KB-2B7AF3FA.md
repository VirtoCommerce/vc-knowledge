BL-LOY-018: A mission grants at most once PER PROGRESS OWNER, and an order contributes at most once `[P0-revenue]`

- **Rule:** Within one progress owner and one progress period, a mission MUST grant its reward at most once, no matter how many times the accrual runs, the progress is re-read, or the order event is redelivered. A given order MUST contribute to a given mission at most once — and this second guarantee is UNCONDITIONAL, holding across owners as well as within one. **The first guarantee is scoped, and the scope is load-bearing:** a mission's progress is keyed on the owner, which is the ORGANIZATION when the store calculates balances per organization and the order carries one, and the USER otherwise. Changing that key — an administrator switching the store's calculation mode, OR a multi-organization buyer simply placing an order under a different company — starts a fresh progress record at zero, which can complete and grant the reward again. The reward is therefore grantable once per distinct owner a customer passes through, not once per customer.
- **Verify:** (a) *Within a scope* — place TWO qualifying orders against a target-2 `OrderCountGoal`: `currentValue` must reach exactly 2 (not 4) and the mission must complete once; re-read the points history repeatedly and confirm an identical entry set with no duplicate row ids. (b) *Across a scope change* — with the store calculating per organization, have one buyer who belongs to two organizations complete the same mission under each: expect (and assert the product's actual intent for) two progress records and two grants. (c) *The unconditional clause* — confirm that no single order appears as a contribution to the same mission under more than one owner.
- **Violation signal:** `currentValue` advancing by more than the order's own contribution; two ledger rows for one mission completion under ONE owner; a re-read producing a larger entry set; one order contributing to the same mission under two owners. **Not a violation of this rule but its declared cost:** the same mission granted once per owner scope a customer traverses.

- **Source:** `Data/Services/LoyaltyMissionLogicService.cs` — `ApplyMissionInternalAsync` short-circuits on a `Completed` status, and `GetOrCreateProgressAsync` searches `criteria.OwnerId` + `PeriodStart`, creating a fresh record at `CurrentValue = 0` on a miss. `ResolveOwnerId(userId, organizationId) => organizationId.IsNullOrEmpty() ? userId : organizationId`, and `ProcessOrderAsync` computes `organizationId = store.IsOrganizationBalanceCalculationMode() ? order.OrganizationId : null` — so the key moves with the STORE MODE and with THE ORDER'S ORGANIZATION independently. The distributed lock is `loyalty-mission:{missionId}:{ownerId}`, also per owner. The database agrees: the unique index is `IX_LoyaltyMissionProgress_MissionId_OwnerId_PeriodStart` (it replaced the per-user `…_MissionId_UserId_PeriodStart` in the organization-balance change), and it constrains nothing across owners. The second clause is implemented separately and deliberately unscoped — `TransactionExistsAsync(missionId, objectId)` is narrowed by neither user nor organization, its own comment stating that an order id is globally unique "so it contributes to a mission exactly once no matter who it is attributed to."
- **Live:** measured 2026-09-14 on a buyer belonging to two organizations. **Within a scope: SATISFIED** — no mission granted twice under any one owner. **Across scopes: the reward multiplied.** The one account held 156 mission-progress rows — 52 under its own user id and 52 under each of its two organizations — with ~20 of the same missions `Completed` in every scope, and the ledger shows **19 missions granted in more than one scope: 38 grants beyond the first, 17,832 points**, each with its own progress id. Two of the three scopes were reached WITHOUT any administrative action: two orders were placed ten seconds apart under one store mode, one against each organization, and the buyer simply switched company. The third followed a mode change — an order carrying an organization contributed to the USER-scope progress, because the mode forces the organization to null. The unconditional clause held throughout: each progress's `currentValue` equalled exactly one order's total, with no order counted under two owners.
- **Status:** **SATISFIED.** The within-owner guarantee holds exactly. The cross-scope multiplication is real and reachable by an ordinary multi-organization buyer with no administrative action — and it is **BY DESIGN** (product decision, 2026-09-14): a mission's reward is earned **once per ORGANIZATION**, not once per customer, so each company accrues from its own qualifying activity into its own pool. Recorded here as a scoped positive oracle: a case may assert the within-scope guarantee as a guarantee, and must NOT assert it across an owner change — an owner change legitimately produces a second grant.
- **The distinction that keeps VCST-5954 a defect under this same model.** Per-organization semantics justify a second grant for a **second organization's own qualifying activity**. They do NOT justify granting an owner rewards for orders placed before that owner existed: on a mode change the new owner's progress completes from the member's HISTORICAL orders, with no new qualifying activity at all. "No new activity" — not "same beneficiary" — is what separates the defect from the designed behaviour, and it is the discriminator a case or a fix should key on.
